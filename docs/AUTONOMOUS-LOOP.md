# Bounded autonomous research loop

This loop is for projects where the human has explicitly authorized repeated,
bounded work.

## Inputs to every iteration

Before selecting work, read:

- the project question and authority boundary;
- the current baseline and success criteria;
- all active, rejected, parked, and obsolete ideas;
- the progress state and stale count;
- the most recent findings and decision log;
- the complete record of any active run.

## Iteration procedure

### 1. Select one direction

Choose the largest unresolved uncertainty or measured bottleneck supported by
completed evidence. Do not select a direction because it sounds plausible
without evidence that it matters.

### 2. Design the cheapest decisive screen

Write the mechanism, method, evidence to preserve, success gate, rejection
gate, resource limit, and stopping condition before execution.

### 3. Execute inside the written boundary

Run only the work authorized by `PROJECT.md`. Preserve raw evidence as it is
created. Do not broaden the project to avoid a failed result.

### 4. Validate proportionally to the claim

A local signal may advance to a wider test. It may not become a general claim
until it survives every required validation stage.

### 5. Record the honest verdict

Use **Successful**, **Rejected**, **Inconclusive**, or **Completed profile**.
Explain what the result establishes and what it does not establish.

### 6. Persist before continuing

Update the run result, findings, idea state, project progress, strongest result,
stale count, and work log. The next iteration must be recoverable from files
alone.

## Diversity rule

One stale iteration records a miss. Two consecutive stale iterations close the
mechanism and require a structural pivot. Changing a number, width, threshold,
or nearby implementation detail is not a structural pivot unless completed
evidence specifically supports it.

## Default session boundary

Unless the project defines another limit, stop after:

- 15 started iterations, or
- 30 minutes of active work,

whichever comes first.

The session boundary is a review checkpoint, not a declaration that the
research objective is complete.

## Stop immediately when

- the human asks to stop;
- the next action exceeds written authority;
- an access or cost boundary is unclear;
- the required evidence cannot be preserved;
- further work would only repeat a closed mechanism;
- the objective has genuinely been achieved.
